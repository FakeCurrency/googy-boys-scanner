## Part 1 — The liquidity floor: stall vs breakout

### Exact current behaviour

One test, one place, applied to VIVEK only. After a name has a confirmed
200-SMA setup (`evaluate` passed) and **before** it is ever scored or graded,
the scan computes its **mean daily turnover over the last 20 completed bars**
(`Close × Volume`; crypto's Yahoo volume is already dollar-volume) and drops
the name if that average is under the market's floor:

| Market | Floor (20-day avg turnover) |
|---|---|
| ASX | A$100,000 / day |
| NASDAQ | US$1,000,000 / day |
| Crypto | US$3,000,000 / day |

Dropped means dropped: not graded, not published, invisible — until this
week's funnel work, uncounted. A second, higher constant (`LIQUID_TIER`: A$1M
/ US$20M / US$100M) only awards a "LIQUID" display tag to published rows; it
gates nothing. The 20-day *average* is the entire test — **a name that just
woke up fails on its sleepy average no matter what today did.** That is the
gap in question.

Two more facts that frame everything:

- **PhaseMap already chose the other philosophy**: its own spec (guardrail 7)
  *tags* thin names ILLIQUID and always shows them — it never drops. The
  VIVEK floor predates the funnel and was never a considered decision at this
  granularity.
- **The bot has its own, separate liquidity defence** (`VIVEK_BOT_MIN_ADV`
  floor plus a position-size-as-%-of-ADV cap), so the scan floor is not the
  only thing standing between a thin name and a paper position — but the bot
  gate reads the row's `adv_usd` field and **skips the check if the field is
  absent**. Any option that publishes thin rows must carry `adv_usd`.

### What the distinction would change — and what it would not

The proposed distinction separates the 20-day *average* (what the name has
been) from *today* (what is arriving). It would **not** change: which setups
exist (detection maths untouched), what the bot may buy (thin rows stay
outside `results` under the proposed rule), Specs, PhaseMap, grades, sizing,
or any published row that passes today's floor. It **would** change only what
you can *see*: the small subset of floor-killed setups where liquidity is
demonstrably arriving would become visible, clearly fenced.

Reading it plainly: **of 299 kills, at most 12 show unusual volume at all

alone.** The other eight "spikes" are 7–18× multiples of *dust*: TRA's 18.4×
day is nine thousand dollars traded. A single A$5,000 position would be
10–55% of the entire day's volume in those names. By any honest test,
**≥98.7% of today's kills are true stalls; the floor is not eating runners
today** — it ate four borderline candidates, several of which (28BB, IHEB,
GOOD, BFL) have the ticker shape of notes/ETF/fund securities the bot never
trades anyway. One known measurement limit: intraday scans read the last
*completed* bar (same determinism rule the grades follow), so "today" firms
up as the session completes.

### The Specs boundary — kept clear

Specs already IS the volume-arrival lens — a ≥3× spike is its *mandatory*
gate — but only under A$0.50 and only as a shortlist generator that never
feeds the bot. The gap this proposal covers is precisely the names **Specs
cannot see**: over 50¢, thin average, volume arriving. Nothing proposed here
touches Specs, and nothing graduates a Specs-style discovery into a bot
candidate.

### Proposed rule (one sentence)

> A floor-killed setup is classed a **breakout candidate** (rather than a
> stall) only when today's turnover *alone* clears the market's floor **and**
> today's volume is ≥3× the name's own 20-day average; such names are
> published to a separate, report-only "liquidity arriving" list — carrying
> `adv_usd`, fund-tagged, capped in count, never entering `results`, never
> visible to the bot — while every other kill stays dropped exactly as now.

(Today that list would have held 4 names. A later, separate decision could
graduate the list into tagged WATCH-capped rows; that would change what gets
taken and is not proposed here.)

### Risk if we get it wrong

The high-multiple/thin-name signature is also the **pump signature** — a
rule keyed on rvol alone would preferentially select manipulated dust, which
is why the today's-turnover-clears-the-floor leg is the load-bearing half.
Execution reality stays hostile even for the four candidates (~1–3% of a
day's volume per A$5k position; spreads in the percent range). If the fence
ever leaks — a thin row reaching `results` without `adv_usd`, or the bot's
fail-open ADV check meeting a row without the field — the failure mode is the
bot sizing into a name it cannot exit, which is why the proposal keeps thin
rows out of `results` entirely rather than trusting tags. Worst case of the
report-only version: the list is noise you learn to ignore — cheap. Worst
case of over-loosening the floor itself: the 8-of-12 dust names above become
"setups" and the funnel's honesty is spent teaching you to distrust the deck.
