## PART A — TURTLE, the fourth lens (shipped this session)

The 1983 Dennis/Eckhardt breakout system on its own tab. Built from the
**Original Turtle Trading Rules**, not the popular short version.

### A1. The two rules the simplified version drops

Both are implemented and both are pinned by named tests.

1. **The System 1 filter.** A 20-day breakout is **skipped** when the previous
   breakout in that market would have been a winner.
   - "Loser" means *price moved 2N against it before a profitable 10-day
     exit*. So a trade that drifted out slightly **below entry** without ever
     going 2N offside counts as a **winner** and blocks the next entry. That
     reads wrong; it is the rule as written.
   - It counts **every breakout the market printed**, taken or skipped. A
     breakout skipped by this rule still becomes "the last breakout".
   - It is **direction-agnostic** — a losing short enables the next long. One
     chronological chain per market, not two.
2. **The 55-day failsafe.** System 2 is never filtered, so a System 1 signal
   blocked by the filter is picked up at 55 days. In the code this falls out of
   testing System 2 *before* System 1 rather than being special-cased.

Dropping (1) turns System 1 into a plain Donchian channel that takes every
whipsaw in a range. That is what the TrendSpider-style version does.

### A2. The complete parameter set

All in `scanner/config.py` (`TURTLE_*`), published in every scan payload as
`params`, and rendered on the page **from that payload** so the prose cannot
describe a system the engine is not running.

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| N | Wilder ATR, period **20**: `N = (19·PDN + TR)/20`, `TR = max(H−L, H−PDC, PDC−L)` |
| System 1 entry / exit | 20-day breakout / 10-day channel |
| System 2 entry / exit | 55-day breakout / 20-day channel |
| Stop | **2N** from the **most recent** unit, applied to the whole position |
| Pyramid | add every **½N** from the last **actual fill**, max **4 units** |
| Unit size | `(1% × account) / (N × dollars-per-point)`; for a share, dollars-per-point = 1 |
| Position limits | 4 per market / 6 closely correlated / 10 loosely / 12 per direction |
| Drawdown rule | cut sizing equity **20% per 10%** of drawdown, **compounding** |
| Whipsaw variant | 0.5% risk at ½N stop; stops **not** raised on adds; re-entry at the original breakout price |
| Entry timing | intraday on the tick, via a resting stop order |

**The number people get wrong:** a full four-unit position risks **5N = 5% of
the account**, not 2%, 4% or 8%. Entries sit at 0, +½N, +1N, +1½N; the single
shared stop lands 2N under the last of them, i.e. ½N *below* the breakout, so
the four units lose ½N + 1N + 1½N + 2N. Left on their own 2N stops they would
lose 8N. Halving that is the entire purpose of the ½N stop raise. Pinned in
both test suites because the page prints the figure.

### A3. How the engine works, and why

`scanner/turtle.py` is a **deterministic bar-by-bar replay**, not a
"does today look like a breakout" test. It has to be: the System 1 filter is a
function of the market's own breakout history, so today's 20-day breakout
cannot be judged without walking the bars before it. A `_Shadow` object runs
beside the real position taking *every* 20-day breakout to keep that memory.

Having paid for the walk, the replay also yields each name's own record under
these rules — which is what the SIGNALS rows show, and it is the equivalent of
TrendSpider's "variance explorer" step.

Non-obvious decisions, all commented in source and pinned by tests:

- `indicators.atr(df, 20)` **IS** N. The rules' recurrence is Wilder at 20, so
  `compute_n` calls the existing function rather than re-typing it. Do not
  "fix" it to a rolling mean — N sets the size, the stop *and* the pyramid
  spacing, so the two smoothings diverge three times over. (Also 20 periods,
  not the 14 most packages default to.)
- Channels are `.shift(1)`. A 20-day high that includes today's own high can
  never be exceeded by it. Entry tests are strictly `>`, so a flat band never
  breaks out; `>=` would fire every day in a dead range.
- **Entry-time N** drives the add spacing, the stop and the size for the whole
  life of a position. Re-reading N each bar would move the rungs under a live
  trade.
- **Exits are checked before adds** within a bar. A daily bar cannot say which
  happened first; this is the conservative reading and can only ever make the
  recorded result worse than reality.
- **Gaps are booked honestly.** An entry fills at `max(level, open)` for a
  long, a stop at `min(stop, open)`. A frame that gaps through its stop books
  the gap, not the stop.
- The entering system **owns the exit** — a 55-day entry does not leave on the
  10-day low just because that level arrives first.

### A4. What the page has (`/turtle.html`)

Four views. **Three of them need no data at all** — a rules reference that
goes blank on a failed fetch is not a reference.

1. **THE RULES** — all eleven rules with formulas, worked tables and the
   reasoning, rendered from the scan's published params.
2. **SIGNALS** — what fired today, what is in a position (with units, average
   fill, the shared stop, the next add level, the exit level and open R), and
   what is approaching a level. Filter by fired / held / approaching, search by
   ticker. Expanding a flat row draws its live pyramid ladder.
3. **SIZING** — a live calculator. Type your account size; unit size, the
   pyramid ladder, the drawdown table, the position limits and the Whipsaw
   comparison all recompute. Stored nowhere, sent nowhere.
4. **EVIDENCE** — the honest arithmetic (see A6).

**The 5-year replay of these rules, per market:**

| Market | Trades | Win rate | Total R | Average R |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ASX | 27,118 | 15.9% | **−5,307R** | **−0.196R** |
| NASDAQ | 44,087 | 17.0% | **−11,118R** | **−0.252R** |
| Crypto | 2,238 | 20.4% | **+3,159R** | **+1.411R** |

**Read this carefully before trading it.** On ASX and NASDAQ equities the
Turtle rules lose money over the last five years — roughly a fifth to a
quarter of an R per trade, across seventy thousand trades. On crypto they make
a great deal. That is exactly the structural point the EVIDENCE view makes:
the Turtles traded ~20 **uncorrelated futures** with margin, and
diversification across uncorrelated markets is the *mechanism* that makes the
expectancy positive, not a garnish. A Turtle system on correlated single
equities is a materially different and worse strategy.

Caveats on those numbers, stated rather than buried: each name is replayed
**alone** with no position limits, no shared capital and no correlation, so
summing them is not a strategy you could have run; the universe is today's
listed names, so it is survivor-biased; and it is in-sample.

### A6. The "5k → 10M" arithmetic, as the page states it

2,000× is **7.60 natural logs**. At a constant rate, nothing withdrawn:

| Annual return | Years to 10M from 5k |
|---|---|
| 30% | 29.0 |
| 50% | 18.7 |
| 80% (the Turtles' reported average) | 12.9 |
| 100% | 11.0 |

And the part usually left out, which the page states plainly: Dennis was
**down ~55% by April 1988** and shut the program; he lost more than half his
assets across 1987–88 (~$50M); **30–50% drawdowns were routine** for
individual Turtles; blended System 1 + System 2 testing produced a worst case
nearer **−80%** than −50%; and the ~80% average is **survivorship-biased**
because poor performers were cut from the program. None of it is publicly
audited.

### A9. Turtle follow-ups NOT done (each is a small, separate piece of work)

1. **Discord ping on Turtle entries.** Deliberately not shipped. The nightly
   already lands counts in the run's step summary, and 30–40 breakouts a day
   into `#alerts` is ambience — and this repo has already paid for the lesson
   that an alarm which cannot stop ringing gets muted. If wanted, the shape is
   the `evidence_brief.yml` Discord step (BOM-trimmed webhook, named UA,

   to a starred watchlist rather than firing on everything.
2. **A Turtle chart overlay.** The video's indicator paints entries/exits/stops
   on the chart (cyan 20-day entry, dark blue 55-day, orange 10-day exit,
   purple 20-day exit, magenta 2N stop, white pyramid numbers). The chart page
   exists (`public/chart.html`); the engine already publishes every level
   needed. This is presentation, not strategy.
3. **A portfolio-level replay.** Every per-name record is a single-instrument
   replay. A true portfolio backtest applying the 4/6/10/12 unit caps, one
   shared equity and the compounding drawdown rule would answer "what would
   this have actually returned" — which the current numbers deliberately do
   not claim to.
4. **Enforcing the correlation limits.** Stated, not enforced, because there is
   no correlation matrix in the repo and sector is a poor proxy. Enforcing them
   would mean inventing a rule the data cannot support.

### B1. The daily edge pipeline (`alert_returns.yml`, 22:20 UTC)

Five scripts in order, each committing only its own artefact:

1. `alert_returns.py` — ingests multi-lens alignments, stamps 1/5/10/20-session
   forward returns into `data/alert_forward_returns.json`, enriches blank-only
   context fields frozen at first write.
2. `edge_rosters.py` — the daily plain-A+ roster **baseline**, on identical
   Yahoo plumbing, so "does alignment beat plain A+?" keeps answering itself.
3. `book_stress.py` — uniform-shock tide table against the real stops →
   `public/data/book_stress.json`.
4. `alert_edge_report.py` — printed into the run's step summary daily.
   Read-only, pinned.
5. `edge_summary.py` — the dedup headline as `public/data/edge_summary.json`.

Plus a **Sunday-only** Discord digest of the report head.

**The design rule that holds it together:** the later scripts *import* the
earlier ones' machinery via importlib. Re-typing a stat or a stamp is
mirror-drift, and tests pin the imports.

### B2. New surfaces, all deliberately inert

- Journal: a quiet **tide line** (how much unrealized R survives −3/−5/−8/−10%
  against real stops), MFE/MAE per row, a † on stop-outs that were wrong from
  entry, per-timeframe and per-mechanism records.
- Deck: a `°` ring on any grade **held by hysteresis** — the tooltip says the
  bot buys `grade_raw`, not the badge.
- Regime: a **breadth-stretch** line — current vs its own mean, midrank
  percentile, and net highs−lows.
- Status sheet: a **"Scan triggers · 7d"** row (cron / manual / self-heal). A
  rising self-heal count is a dying cron wearing a green lamp.

### B3. The headline research findings (all with real numbers)

From `EDGE_RESEARCH_2026-08-20.md`:

- **A+ LONG selection is the measurable edge**: ~+1%/week of excess over the
  median name, replicating independently in both markets at ~3 SE.
- **The ⨂ aligned badge adds nothing detectable** in aggregate (+0.18% vs
  +0.12%, on a combined SE of ~0.37pp).
- **The short side was anti-predictive** in this window: −1.56%/5s (ASX) and
  −0.96% (NASDAQ) signed excess, *after* removing the tide.
- **The whole realized bleed is 1D-entry full stops**: all 7 rules-side stops
  were 1D entries, and 3 of 7 never ticked positive at all (MFE = 0).
- **The 28-day time-stop is the one mechanism that works**: +2.14R over 11
  exits. The instinct to "fix" it first would aim at the only profitable part.
- **Score digits are noise** at 5 sessions (Spearman ρ = +0.012, non-monotonic).
- **🎯 High conviction underperforms**: +0.36% vs +1.44% on longs.
- **The deck's green reclaim tint reads a 10-year file the live book
  contradicts at 3.6 SE.**

Security, integrity and observability. Highlights worth knowing:

  4-day TTL, no request bodies, coalesced GETs).
- **`commit_sentinel.yml`** — detection half of branch protection: checks the
  authenticated pusher and every commit identity against main's real history.
  Detection only; anomaly = green run + Discord warning.
- **Auth on `/api/close`, `/api/scan`, `/api/journal` is a Cloudflare Access
  decision, not a secret** — all three are called from browser JS, so a shared
  secret would ship in page source and protect nothing. **Still pending your
  configuration**; the access log is the compensating control until then.

  committed directory's newest snapshot age. Do not drop the commit step
  without rewiring the watchdog.
